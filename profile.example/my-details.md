# My real details for the interview

Write short bullet points in English under each heading; real numbers beat polished sentences.
Leave a section empty if it doesn't apply to you. You can also fill these in on the app's
My details page.

The app reads this file: the mock interviewer digs into these points, and the interview overlay
shows them as your cues. Keep each `## [id]` heading line exactly as it is. Lines starting with `>`
are hints and are ignored.

## [project-actions] Your best project: what did YOU do?
> How you split the work, what you did yourself, and one problem or disagreement you resolved.
> Expect "What exactly did you do yourself?"


## [project-results] That project's results, in numbers
> Time saved, users, accuracy, cost, a score or grade: anything measurable.


## [project-mistake] One honest mistake and its lesson
> For "What went wrong?" and "What would you do differently?"


## [root-cause] A problem you diagnosed (root-cause story)
> A time results were wrong or unstable. What you checked first, what you ruled out, the cause,
> the fix, and how you confirmed it.


## [skill-example] One piece of work that shows your skills
> For "Academic background and professional skills": a dataset you cleaned, a design you built,
> an experiment you ran, and what it showed.


## [research-status] Your research or main project: real status and numbers
> What it is in plain words, and where you are now. Don't claim results you can't show.


## [chinese-study] How you study Chinese
> Classes, apps, how many minutes a day, what you can already say.


## [tocfl-plan] When and where you'll take TOCFL
> Target level, month and test place.


## [stay-years] How long you plan to stay in Taiwan
> The program requires at least 2 years of work after graduation, so managers treat 2 as the minimum.


## [stress] How you really cope with stress, and how often
> Your real method, plus one hard week and what you did.


## [overtime] A time you worked extra to hit a deadline
> For "Are you willing to work overtime or shifts?"


## [weakness] Your strengths, and a real weakness with an example
> A good weakness is the other side of a strength. Add one real example and what you do about it now.


## [different] What makes you different
> Your rare mix of skills or experience, and one moment that shows it.


## [taiwan-likes] Two specific things you like about Taiwan
> A food, a person who helped you, a place, or what you look forward to and why.
> Not "everything" or "the people".


## [challenge] Your biggest challenge or setback
> What happened, what you did, what you learned.


## [quick-learning] One example of learning something fast
> For "You have little work experience. Why are you qualified?"


## [double-degree] How your degrees work together (if you're in a double-degree program)
> Credit transfer, thesis supervision, timeline.


## [my-questions] The two questions you'll ask them
> Pick from the "Your questions" card or write your own; keep one or two in reserve.
